2 Piro Restoration Plots

Introduction: While active restoration strategies can be a powerful approach to restoring forests, it is usually a costly approach to worldwide forest restoration programs. Thus, it becomes necessary to shed light on how different restoration approaches benefit biodiversity recovery. Under such circumstances, biodiversity studies on restoring ecosystems in developing tropical countries are mandatory, and thus, restoration experiments in these regions are exceptionally valuable. The objective of this research study is to test the efficacy of different restoration approaches on the biodiversity in a Neotropical forest of Central America. We tested the efficacy of several different forest restoration strategies within the Osa Peninsula of southwestern Costa Rica to restore abandoned cattle pasture.

The restoration experiment comprises forty 0.5-ha experimental plots. The plots were established and planted in 2017 and 2018. Forty plots were originally divided into four treatments, each with 10 replicates based on planting regimes of pioneer species and a natural regeneration control. In addition to the experimental restoration plots, we survey 10 mature forest recording sites in old growth forest and in 50 year old secondary growth forest.
