7 Farms for Nature

Introduction: Because monitoring all aspects of biodiversity is logistically and financially prohibitive, umbrella, flagship, and indicator species were developed as a conservation strategy to monitor and protect entire communities using a single-species approach (Simberloff 1998). These species are generally wide-ranging and charismatic, and their presence is believed to reflect ecosystem health (Simberloff 1998; McGowan et al. 2020). Therefore, we will use the presence of three indicator groups to reflect changes and improvements in biodiversity on individual farms. The three groups are felids, game birds, and large herbivores (i.e., tapirs, Tapirus bairdii, or white-lipped peccaries, Tayassu pecari), and their presence would imply improvements in ecological and habitat conditions. We expect the presence of these indicator species to increase throughout the 5-year monitoring period provided the restoration areas are adequately managed and restoration efforts are successful. The number of groups detected on farms will determine the number of credits granted to farmers.

The goal of the project is to quantify biodiversity “gains” on farms participating in the Farms for Nature program as a way to monitor restoration success and the impact of improved farming practices and interventions at the farm level on enhancing biodiversity.
